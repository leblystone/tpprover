## Introduction

Tracking your peptide research properly is essential for monitoring protocols, identifying patterns, and maintaining consistent records. Whether you're researching GLP-1 peptides like Semaglutide and Tirzepatide, or other research peptides, having a systematic tracking method ensures accurate dose logging and helps you stay organized.

In this guide, we'll cover the best methods for tracking peptide dosages, what information to log, and how to create an effective tracking system.

---

## Why Track Peptide Dosages?

### 1. **Maintain Protocol Consistency**
Consistent tracking helps you follow your research protocol accurately. Missing doses or incorrect dosages can affect your research results and make it difficult to identify patterns.

### 2. **Monitor Side Effects and Reactions**
By logging dosages alongside observations and side effects, you can identify correlations between doses and any reactions. This is especially important when titrating doses or trying new peptides.

### 3. **Track Reconstitution Dates**
Many peptides have limited shelf life after reconstitution. Tracking mixing dates and expiration dates prevents using expired peptides and ensures research safety.

### 4. **Organize Multiple Peptides**
If you're researching multiple peptides simultaneously, a tracking system helps you keep protocols separate, prevent mix-ups, and manage your stockpile inventory.

### 5. **Historical Reference**
Your tracking log becomes a valuable reference for future research cycles, helping you remember what worked, what didn't, and how you adjusted protocols over time.

---

## What Information Should You Track?

### Essential Tracking Data:

**1. Date and Time**
- Record the exact date and time of each injection
- Helps maintain consistent timing (especially for once-weekly GLP-1s)
- Useful for identifying timing-related patterns

**2. Peptide Name and Dosage**
- Full peptide name (e.g., "Semaglutide" not just "Sema")
- Exact dose amount (e.g., "0.5mg" or "250mcg")
- Vial concentration (e.g., "5mg/2ml")

**3. Injection Site**
- Location (abdomen, thigh, upper arm, etc.)
- Rotate injection sites to prevent lipohypertrophy
- Note any site reactions or bruising

**4. Reconstitution Information**
- Date mixed
- Reconstitution volume (e.g., "2ml bacteriostatic water")
- Expiration date (typically 30 days for most peptides)
- Vial number (if tracking multiple vials)

**5. Observations and Side Effects**
- Any immediate reactions
- Side effects throughout the day
- Changes in appetite, energy, or other relevant metrics
- Overall feeling/tolerance

**6. Research Notes**
- Protocol adjustments
- Reasons for dose changes
- Research goals and milestones
- Questions or concerns to research further

## Step-by-Step: How to Track Peptide Dosages

### Step 1: Choose Your Tracking Method
Select the method that fits your lifestyle. Many researchers use a combination (e.g., physical planner for daily tracking + spreadsheet for long-term analysis).

### Step 2: Set Up Your Tracking Template
Create columns or sections for all essential data:
- Date/Time
- Peptide name
- Dosage
- Injection site
- Reconstitution date
- Observations
- Notes

### Step 3: Log EVERY Injection Immediately
Don't wait until the end of the day. Log your injection right after administering it. This ensures accuracy and prevents forgetting details.

### Step 4: Review Weekly
At the end of each week, review your tracking log:
- Are you following your protocol consistently?
- Any patterns in side effects?
- Do you need to adjust dosages?
- Are any vials approaching expiration?

### Step 5: Plan Ahead
Use your tracking log to plan future research:
- When to reconstitute new vials
- When to reorder peptides
- Protocol adjustments for next cycle

## Tracking GLP-1 Peptides (Semaglutide, Tirzepatide, Retatrutide)

GLP-1 peptides have unique tracking needs due to dose escalation protocols:

### Dose Escalation Tracking
Most GLP-1 protocols start at low doses and gradually increase:

**Example Semaglutide Protocol:**
- Week 1-4: 0.25mg once weekly
- Week 5-8: 0.5mg once weekly
- Week 9-12: 1.0mg once weekly
- Week 13+: Adjust based on tolerance

**Track:**
- ✅ Current week of protocol
- ✅ Target dose for current week
- ✅ Actual dose administered
- ✅ Tolerance and side effects at each dose level
- ✅ When to escalate to next dose

### Side Effect Monitoring
GLP-1s commonly cause:
- Nausea (especially when escalating doses)
- Appetite suppression
- Fatigue
- Digestive changes

**Track these daily to identify:**
- Which dose levels cause side effects
- When side effects peak (usually 1-3 days post-injection)
- How long side effects last
- Whether you need to slow escalation

## Common Tracking Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. **Inconsistent Logging**
❌ Logging some days but not others  
✅ Log EVERY injection without exception

### 2. **Not Recording Reconstitution Dates**
❌ Forgetting when you mixed vials  
✅ Always note mixing date and calculate expiration

### 3. **Vague Observations**
❌ "Felt off today"  
✅ "Mild nausea 2 hours post-injection, resolved by evening"

### 4. **No Site Rotation Tracking**
❌ Injecting same spot repeatedly  
✅ Track injection sites and rotate systematically

### 5. **Waiting Too Long to Log**
❌ Trying to remember yesterday's injection  
✅ Log immediately after injecting
