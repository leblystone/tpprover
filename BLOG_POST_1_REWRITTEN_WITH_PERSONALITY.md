## What You Actually Need to Track (No BS Version)

Skip the 47-column spreadsheet. Here's what matters:

### The Non-Negotiables:

**1. Date & Time**
- When did you inject?
- Not "morning"—actual time
- Future you will thank present you

**2. What & How Much**
- Peptide name (full name, not "sema")
- Exact dose ("0.5mg" not "half-ish")
- Which vial (if you have multiple)

**3. Where You Stuck It**
- Injection site matters
- Rotating prevents lumps and bruising
- "Abdomen" isn't specific enough when you're 12 weeks in

**4. When You Mixed It**
- Reconstitution date
- Expiration date (usually 30 days)
- Bacteriostatic water volume
- **Label your damn vials**

**5. How You Felt**
- Side effects (nausea, fatigue, etc.)
- Energy levels
- Appetite changes
- Anything weird

**6. Why You Changed Anything**
- Dose adjustments
- Timing changes
- What prompted it
- (So you don't repeat mistakes)

## How to Track GLP-1s Without Losing Your Mind

GLP-1 peptides (Semaglutide, Tirzepatide, Retatrutide) are special little snowflakes because:
- They have dose escalation protocols
- Side effects are dose-dependent
- Once-weekly dosing (easy to forget)
- Long-term tracking is essential

### What to Track for GLP-1s:

**Dose Escalation Timeline:**
```
Week 1-4: 0.25mg
Week 5-8: 0.5mg
Week 9-12: 1.0mg
(Your protocol may vary)
```

Track:
- ✅ Which week you're on
- ✅ Current target dose
- ✅ Actual dose you took
- ✅ How you tolerated it
- ✅ When to escalate next

**Side Effect Tracking:**
GLP-1 side effects follow patterns:
- Usually peak 1-3 days post-injection
- Lessen with continued use
- Worsen when escalating doses

**What to note:**
- Day 1 post-injection: How do you feel?
- Day 2-3: Side effects peaking?
- Day 4-7: Returning to baseline?
- Next injection: Ready or need to slow down?

**The Key Question:** Are you building tolerance or getting wrecked? This determines if you escalate or stay at current dose.

## The Mistakes That Will Cost You Money

### Mistake #1: Not Labeling Reconstituted Vials
**What happens:** Mystery vials in your fridge. Is this 2.5mg/ml or 5mg/ml? Is it even BPC-157 or is it the TB-500?

**The fix:** 
- Label IMMEDIATELY when reconstituting
- Include: Peptide name, concentration, recon date, expiration
- Use permanent marker
- Don't trust future you to "just remember"

### Mistake #2: Forgetting Reconstitution Dates
**What happens:** You inject peptide that's been sitting reconstituted for 87 days. Congratulations, you just wasted $50.

**The fix:**
- Track recon date
- Set calendar reminders for expiration
- Most peptides = 30 days refrigerated after reconstitution
- When in doubt, throw it out (RIP money, but better than injecting dead peptide)

### Mistake #3: Vague Observations
**Don't write:**
"Felt off today"

**Do write:**
"Moderate nausea 2-3 hours post-injection, resolved by evening. Appetite suppression stronger than last week. Energy dip around 4 PM."

**Why it matters:** Pattern recognition. "Felt off" tells you nothing. Specific observations tell you whether to adjust timing, dose, or injection site.

### Mistake #4: Not Tracking Injection Sites
**What happens:** You inject in the same spot repeatedly. Now you have lumps, bruising, or lipohypertrophy.

**The fix:**
- Rotate systematically
- Track where you injected last time
- Use different quadrants of abdomen/thighs
- Give each site at least a week to recover

### Mistake #5: Only Tracking on Injection Days
**What happens:** You miss patterns that occur on off-days (especially important for once-weekly GLP-1s).

**The fix:**
- Brief daily check-ins
- Even non-injection days: Energy? Appetite? Side effects?
- Helps identify patterns (e.g., side effects peak day 2-3 post-injection)

## Real Talk: Tracking vs. Obsessing

**Here's the balance:**

### Track This: ✅
- Every injection (date, time, dose, site)
- Reconstitution dates
- Significant side effects
- Changes to protocols
- Weekly reflections on progress

### Don't Obsess Over: ❌
- Hour-by-hour symptom updates
- Minor fluctuations in everything
- Creating a 47-page spreadsheet
- Tracking for tracking's sake

**The goal:** Useful data that helps you optimize protocols. Not performative productivity theater.

## The Lazy Person's Minimum Viable Tracking System

If you're not going to track everything (and let's be honest, you might not), at MINIMUM track:

1. **Date + Time** of injection
2. **Peptide + Dose**
3. **Reconstitution date** (on the vial label)
4. **Major side effects**

That's it. Four things. If you track nothing else, track these four.

Will it be perfect? No.  
Will it be infinitely better than nothing? Yes.
