## Introduction

Reconstituting peptides correctly is one of the most important skills for peptide researchers. Mix them wrong, and you could end up with incorrect dosages or degraded peptides. But with the right formula and careful technique, reconstitution is straightforward.

In this guide, we'll walk through the reconstitution process step-by-step, provide calculator formulas, and share best practices for mixing peptides safely.

---

## What is Peptide Reconstitution?

**Reconstitution** is the process of mixing lyophilized (freeze-dried) peptide powder with bacteriostatic water to create an injectable solution.

### Why Peptides Come Lyophilized:
- ✅ Longer shelf life (1-2 years vs 30 days reconstituted)
- ✅ More stable during shipping
- ✅ Easier to measure precise amounts
- ✅ Flexible dosing (you choose the concentration)

### Reconstitution Basics:
- **Lyophilized peptide** (powder in vial) + **Bacteriostatic water** = **Injectable solution**
- Example: 5mg peptide + 2ml water = 2.5mg/ml solution

---

## What You'll Need

### Materials Required:
1. ✅ Lyophilized peptide vial
2. ✅ Bacteriostatic water (NOT sterile water—bac water prevents bacterial growth)
3. ✅ Alcohol wipes
4. ✅ Syringe (1ml-3ml for mixing, insulin syringe for injecting)
5. ✅ Labels and marker (for marking vial with date and concentration)

### Before You Begin:
- Wash hands thoroughly
- Clean work surface
- Let refrigerated peptides come to room temperature (prevents condensation)
- Have your dosage calculations ready

---

## The Reconstitution Formula

### Basic Formula:
```
(Peptide Amount in mg / Reconstitution Volume in ml) = Concentration in mg/ml
```

### To Calculate Injection Volume:
```
(Desired Dose in mg / Concentration in mg/ml) = Volume to Inject in ml
```

### Example 1: Semaglutide (5mg vial)

**Goal:** Create 2.5mg/ml concentration for easy 0.25mg dosing

- 5mg ÷ 2ml = 2.5mg/ml concentration

**Step 2: Calculate Injection Volume for 0.25mg Dose**
- 0.25mg ÷ 2.5mg/ml = 0.1ml (10 units on insulin syringe)

## Step-by-Step Reconstitution Process

### Step 1: Prepare Your Workspace
1. Clean work surface with alcohol wipe
2. Lay out all materials
3. Wash hands thoroughly

### Step 2: Prepare the Vials
1. Remove caps from peptide and bacteriostatic water vials
2. Wipe both rubber stoppers with alcohol wipes
3. Let alcohol dry completely (prevents peptide degradation)

### Step 3: Draw Bacteriostatic Water
1. Insert syringe needle into bacteriostatic water vial
2. Draw your calculated volume (e.g., 2ml)
3. Check for air bubbles and expel them
4. Remove syringe from vial

### Step 4: Add Water to Peptide (CAREFULLY!)
**CRITICAL: Do NOT inject water directly onto the peptide powder!**

1. Insert needle into peptide vial at an angle
2. Touch needle to the glass wall of the vial
3. **Slowly inject water down the side of the vial**
4. Let water gently slide down and mix with powder
5. Remove syringe

### Step 5: Gentle Mixing
1. **Do NOT shake!** Shaking can denature peptides
2. Gently swirl the vial in circular motions
3. Let it sit for 2-3 minutes
4. Continue gentle swirling until powder is fully dissolved
5. Solution should be clear (slight cloudiness may be normal for some peptides)

### Step 6: Label and Store
1. Label vial with:
   - Peptide name
   - Concentration (e.g., "2.5mg/ml")
   - Reconstitution date
   - Expiration date (typically 30 days)
2. Store in refrigerator immediately (2-8°C)
3. Keep away from light

## Common Reconstitution Mistakes

### 1. **Injecting Water Directly Onto Powder**
❌ Forceful injection can damage peptide structure  
✅ Inject slowly down the side of the vial

### 2. **Shaking the Vial**
❌ Vigorous shaking can denature peptides  
✅ Gentle swirling only

### 3. **Using Wrong Water**
❌ Sterile water (no preservative, bacteria can grow)  
❌ Tap water (contains minerals, bacteria)  
✅ Bacteriostatic water only

### 4. **Not Labeling the Vial**
❌ Forgetting concentration or reconstitution date  
✅ Label immediately with all critical info

### 5. **Storing at Room Temperature**
❌ Peptides degrade quickly when warm  
✅ Refrigerate immediately after reconstitution

### 6. **Reusing Needles**
❌ Dull needles, contamination risk  
✅ Use fresh needle for each injection

## How Long Do Reconstituted Peptides Last?

### General Guidelines:

**Most Peptides:**
- 🧊 Refrigerated (2-8°C): **30 days**

- **GLP-1s (Semaglutide, Tirzepatide):** 30 days refrigerated
- **BPC-157, TB-500:** 30 days refrigerated

## FAQs

**Q: Can I use sterile water instead of bacteriostatic water?**  
A: Only if you're using the entire vial within 24 hours. Bacteriostatic water contains preservatives that prevent bacterial growth for 30 days.

**Q: My peptide won't dissolve completely. What should I do?**  
A: Let it sit in the fridge for 30 minutes, then gently swirl again. Some peptides take longer to dissolve. If it's still cloudy after 1 hour, contact your vendor.

**Q: Can I freeze reconstituted peptides?**  
A: This is debated. Some researchers freeze pre-loaded syringes, but freezing may degrade certain peptides. Refrigeration is always safer.

**Q: How many times can I puncture the rubber stopper?**  
A: Generally 30-50 times. After that, the stopper degrades and may contaminate the solution.

**Q: What if I accidentally shake the vial?**  
A: Your peptide is probably fine. Just avoid vigorous shaking in the future. Gentle swirling is always better.
